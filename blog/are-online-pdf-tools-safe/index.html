What Actually Happens When You Upload a PDF Online?

When you click "Upload" on a traditional PDF website, your file leaves your machine and travels to an external cloud server. Behind the scenes, these websites must store your document on their disk drives to execute command-line tools like Ghostscript, QPDF, or Poppler.

Even if the website’s privacy policy states that files are deleted after one hour:

  • Cloud backup snapshots may retain copies on backup storage servers for months.
  • Misconfigured Amazon S3 buckets can expose millions of uploaded customer files publicly to the internet.
  • Free tools often monetize user traffic through third-party analytics trackers and behavioral profiling networks.

The Rise of Zero-Upload, Client-Side Tools

Fortunately, web architecture has evolved. Thanks to WebAssembly (WASM), browsers can now run desktop-grade compilation code directly on your computer’s local processor.

CreatorNew was engineered from the ground up around a strict **Zero-Upload Philosophy**: all image conversions, PDF merges, background cutouts, and compressions execute 100% within your local browser sandbox. We never see your documents, we cannot read your files, and we cannot store your data.

Checklist: How to Audit Any Online Tool for Privacy

  1. Check the Network Tab: Open DevTools (F12) > Network. When you process a file, does the network tab show a multi-megabyte POST request uploading your file? If yes, it is a cloud server tool.
  2. Test Offline: Turn off your Wi-Fi after loading the page. If the tool still operates, it runs client-side!
  3. Read the Privacy Policy: Avoid tools that claim the right to inspect or use your uploaded content for service improvements.
