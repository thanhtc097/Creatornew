When PNG Is Superior to JPG for PDF Conversion

While JPG is fantastic for continuous-tone photography, it struggles with sharp contrast transitions—such as black text on a clean white background. JPEG compression groups pixels into 8x8 blocks, creating faint "mosquito noise" around character edges.

PNG avoids this entirely. Because PNG uses lossless compression, every letter, line, and diagram coordinate is preserved with pixel-perfect precision. It is the gold standard for converting:

  • Architectural blueprints and CAD drawings
  • Financial spreadsheets and balance sheet tables
  • Music scores and musical tablature
  • Logos and brand guideline documents

Frequently Asked Questions

Does converting to PNG preserve transparent backgrounds from the PDF?

Standard PDF documents have an implicit white page background. However, vector elements without background fills can be rendered with alpha transparency if specified.

Why are converted PNG files larger than JPG files?

PNG is a lossless format that preserves every pixel without compression shortcuts. For maximum clarity on text and line art, the slightly larger file size is well worth the visual fidelity.
