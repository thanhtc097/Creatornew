When You Should Use JPG / JPEG

JPG is the uncontested champion for **photographs and complex continuous-tone imagery** where every pixel contains a subtly different hue:
  • E-commerce product photography portfolios
  • Full-bleed hero banner images on desktop websites
  • Email marketing campaigns sent to older desktop Outlook clients
  • Print production workflows requiring CMYK color models
Never use JPG for logos, graphics with text, or images requiring transparent backgrounds.

When You Should Use PNG

PNG remains the standard for **precise digital graphics and lossless master files**:
  • Transparent company logos and website header branding
  • Software UI screenshots where menu text must remain crisp and readable
  • Flat vector illustrations, chart infographics, and diagram graphics
  • Images that undergo repeated editing and saving without generation loss

Why WebP Is the Default Choice for Modern Webmasters in 2026

Developed by Google, WebP merges the best attributes of both JPG and PNG into a single modern container:

  • It supports lossy compression that is 25% to 34% smaller than JPEG at equivalent SSIM visual quality.
  • It supports lossless compression that is 26% smaller than PNG.
  • It supports full 8-bit alpha transparency even in lossy mode—something JPG can never do.

Unless you are printing physical brochures or emailing legacy corporate networks, **WebP should be your default delivery format** on the web.
