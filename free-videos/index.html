YouTube Creator Blueprint

How to use open stock footage without copyright strikes

Protect your YouTube channel from Content ID claims and false automated copyright strikes.

Why Stock Footage Gets Claimed: YouTube's automated Content ID system scans uploaded video frames against a fingerprint database. Sometimes, third-party creators or multi-channel networks (MCNs) take open-access public domain or Creative Commons footage from Wikimedia Commons, upload it, and mistakenly enroll it into Content ID. Having your source documentation ready lets you overturn false claims effortlessly.

1. Verify That 'Modifications Allowed' is Checked

When you edit a video clip into your YouTube timeline—trimming the length, adding color grading, adding background music, or zooming in—you are creating a derivative work. Never use clips with the ND (NoDerivatives) restriction in your video edits. On CreatorNew, use the filter dropdown to select "Modifications allowed" to ensure your edits are 100% legally compliant.

2. Paste Full Video Attribution into Your Description

Creative Commons BY licenses require reasonable attribution. Simply click the Copy attribution button on any video card on CreatorNew, then paste the credit into your YouTube video description:

📹 Video Footage Credits:
Clip: [Video Title] by [Creator / Wikimedia Contributor]
License: Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY)
Source: [Wikimedia Commons Source URL]

3. Save the Wikimedia Commons Source URL

Always keep a spreadsheet or note of the Wikimedia Commons source URL for every stock clip used in your video project. The Wikimedia page displays the exact upload date, license badge, and original author, serving as indisputable legal proof of your right to use the footage.

4. How to Dispute a False Video Claim in YouTube Studio

  1. Navigate to YouTube Studio > Content and locate the flagged video.
  2. In the Restrictions column, click Copyright > See Details.
  3. Click Select Action > Dispute.
  4. Select "License: I have permission or a license to use this content".
  5. Confirm that the content is licensed under Creative Commons or Public Domain.
  6. In the explanation box, paste the clip title, creator name, exact Creative Commons license version, and the Wikimedia Commons URL.
  7. Submit. The claimant has 30 days to respond. In virtually all open-license disputes, the automated claim is released within 48 hours without affecting your channel standing.

Frequently asked questions

Are the videos on CreatorNew free to download and use?

Yes. All stock videos are indexed from open media repositories like Wikimedia Commons under Creative Commons or Public Domain licenses with zero watermark overlays.

Can I use these videos as B-roll in monetized YouTube and TikTok videos?

Yes. Creative Commons videos can be used in monetized content as long as the license permits commercial use (such as CC0, CC BY, or CC BY-SA) and you give proper attribution when required.

Can I edit, crop, and color grade these video clips?

Yes, provided the clip does not contain the 'ND' (NoDerivatives) restriction. All clips matching our 'Modifications allowed' filter can be trimmed, color graded, and remixed freely.

How do I avoid copyright strikes when using Creative Commons footage on YouTube?

Always ensure the license allows commercial derivatives, paste the exact attribution line into your YouTube description, and archive the source Wikimedia link to overturn any automated bot claims via YouTube Studio dispute.

How do I credit the video author properly?

Every video card provides a one-click 'Copy Attribution' button that gives you the standardized credit line including clip title, creator name, license type, and source URL.

Do I need an account, subscription, or payment to download videos?

No account, payment, or subscription is ever required. You can download clips directly in your browser with zero limits.
