YouTube Creator Blueprint

How to use free music on YouTube without copyright strikes

Avoid false Content ID claims, revenue sharing hijacking, and video takedowns with these proven practices.

The Content ID Dilemma: Automated music fingerprinting algorithms on YouTube frequently flag royalty-free classical recordings or Creative Commons tracks because third-party distributors upload similar recordings to rights registries. Following proper licensing protocol gives you 100% legal backing to overturn any claim instantly.

1. Filter for CC0 or CC BY Before Downloading

If your YouTube channel is in the YouTube Partner Program (YPP), monetizes with Google AdSense, features affiliate links, or includes brand sponsorships, you are legally considered a commercial creator. Never use tracks with the 'NC' (Non-Commercial) tag in monetized videos. Use CreatorNew's license dropdown to filter exclusively for Free use (CC0 / Public Domain) or CC BY.

2. Paste Full Attribution in Your YouTube Video Description

Creative Commons BY licenses require reasonable attribution. Simply click the Copy attribution button on any audio card in CreatorNew. Then paste the credit into your YouTube video description under a clear heading:

🎵 Audio Credits:
Track: [Track Title] by [Artist / Creator Name]
License: Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY)
Source: [Openverse / CreatorNew Landing URL]

3. Save Proof of the Original License

When you download a track, bookmark or save the foreign landing URL (the external source link provided on each track card). If an automated rights administrator ever files a Content ID claim against your video, having the original source URL showing the Creative Commons badge will resolve the dispute in under 48 hours.

4. How to Dispute a False Content ID Claim Step-by-Step

  1. Go to YouTube Studio > Content and locate the claimed video.
  2. Hover over Copyright in the Restrictions column and click See Details.
  3. Click Actions > Dispute.
  4. Select "I have a license or permission to use this content".
  5. Provide your explanation citing the track title, creator, Creative Commons license version, and paste the source URL.
  6. Submit the dispute. During the dispute, your monetization revenue is held in escrow and paid out to you once the claim is released.

Frequently asked questions

Are the sounds and music on CreatorNew free to use?

Yes. All audio tracks are indexed from open collections via Openverse and published under Creative Commons licenses or in the public domain.

Can I use these sounds in monetized YouTube videos, podcasts, and games?

Yes, provided you select tracks licensed under CC0 (Public Domain), CC BY (Attribution), or CC BY-SA (ShareAlike). Do not use tracks marked with NC (Non-Commercial) if you earn ad revenue, sponsorships, or sell your project.

How do I avoid YouTube copyright strikes and Content ID claims?

To prevent claims, always choose CC0 or CC BY audio, paste the complete credit line into your video description, and save the source landing page URL as proof of your legal license in case of automated bot claims.

What is the difference between CC0, CC BY, and CC BY-SA?

CC0 is Public Domain dedication (use freely with zero attribution). CC BY permits commercial and personal use but requires crediting the creator. CC BY-SA requires you to distribute any derivative remix under the exact same license.

What should I do if YouTube gives me a false Content ID claim?

Click 'Dispute' in YouTube Studio, select 'I have a license or permission to use this content', and paste the track title, creator name, Creative Commons license type, and the original Openverse source URL. Legitimate CC claims are released within 24–48 hours.

Do I need an account or subscription to download sounds?

No. You can search, preview, and download audio tracks directly without registration, monthly fees, or download limits.
